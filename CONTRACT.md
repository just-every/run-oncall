# Oncall product contract

Target edition · 16 October 2026 · US-first · USD.

Oncall owns the full on-call alerting and incident-response job: receive monitoring or phone-in incidents, identify coverage, wake a responder through real phone/SMS/push, escalate until acknowledged, coordinate verified recovery, communicate and finish the follow-up. The first buyer is a US software/IT operations team replacing PagerDuty, Opsgenie / Jira Service Management incident response, or Splunk On-Call (VictorOps). The target also competes with incident.io On-call, Rootly and Resolve AI through integrated response and bounded agent work. This is a destination contract, not a description of what currently ships.

The approved 16 October scope governs these clauses. Release 1 remains hosted coordination beside the existing pager, with production paging OFF. Writing a target does not activate providers, change the approved UI/homepage or waive the independent standby and real-phone cutover requirements. Existing IDs remain stable. Clauses OC-001–022 retain release guarantees; OC-023 onward completes the purchased job. A clause is **done** only within its stated limits and cited exercising flow; **partial** names the implemented subset; **missing** has no implementing flow. Acceptance evidence is the public journey required to complete the clause; merely listing it is not a passing run.

## Preserved coordination guarantees

- **OC-001 — Event identity and alerts.** Persist accepted events separately from alerts and human incidents. Repeated source event IDs return the original receipt; changed bytes with the same identity conflict. A deduplication key groups active alerts only within its source/service/environment; recovery affects the matching alert and never resolves an incident.

  **Acceptance evidence:** POST signed and bearer events through /api/ingress/:source; repeat a normalized identity, conflict changed content, send recovery before trigger and after a newer generation, and inspect protected state. Verify grouping across multiple sources without merging service/environment boundaries.

- **OC-002 — Routing replay.** Retain routing policy version and explanation at admission. Replay is read-only, returns ordered decisions, and produces no alert, incident or external effect. Unmatched signals remain visible and unowned; suppression reasons remain inspectable.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Admit a severity-routed event, change policy, POST /api/replay and compare the immutable admission explanation with the proposed decision in submitted order; compare state before/after for zero writes.

- **OC-003 — Roles and authority.** Incident changes require an active authorized session. Viewer reads; responder coordinates; admin manages services/policies/members/import. Ingest credentials grant no read or human authority. Bearer and signed intake return only `duplicate`, stored event `id`, source `eventId`, `service`, `observedAt` and `acceptedAt` (201 accepted; 200 deduplicated). Routing decisions, snapshots and alert identities stay behind session-protected read/export doors. Revocation takes effect on the next request. Every mutation enforces current authority server-side. The stakeholder role that reads only business-service status and approved updates is specified and tracked in OC-061.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use anonymous, expired, revoked, viewer, responder and admin sessions on reads, commands, sources and archives; revoke or downgrade while a real HTTP mutation is incomplete and prove no publication. Verify minimal intake receipts disclose no private routing data.

- **OC-004 — Incident state transitions.** Incident state transitions are explicit. Acknowledgement assigns responsibility. Resolution requires recovery evidence and a summary, independently of alert recovery. Optimistic revision checks reject stale edits. All accepted changes append an attributed timeline transactionally with state. This resolution rule governs declared incidents; an alert-only page that its source recovers closes without a human summary under OC-056.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Declare from admitted alerts, acknowledge, reject stale concurrent changes, clear the source alert, refuse resolution without evidence and summary, then resolve and inspect the attributed timeline over HTTP.

- **OC-005 — Copied runbooks.** Runbooks are copied into the incident at declaration; future service edits never silently rewrite incident instructions. Follow-ups carry owner, due date, open/completed state and completion evidence. Resolved incidents retain open work and editable review.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Declare an incident, edit its service runbook and confirm the copied incident steps remain unchanged; create an owned due-dated action, require completion evidence and retain unfinished work and review after resolution.

- **OC-006 — No outbound incident effects.** No paging provider notification, AI call, production action or Lamp publication occurs in release 1. Configured remote mail sends sign-in links only. Handoffs are minimum summaries with stable identity and visibility; exported draft is explicitly unreviewed. Source links grant no access. External acceptance/delivery/acknowledgement are distinct later records.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use coordination and handoff preparation/export on a normal installation; prove no paging receipt, production action, AI call or external publication occurs. Confirm draft exports remain unreviewed and source links confer no access.

- **OC-007 — Native archive.** Native archive preserves services, policies, incident/alert/event/timeline identity and attribution, runbook and follow-up history. Validate schema and references before atomic restore. Invalid restore preserves existing data. Do not export authentication secrets/sessions. Released migrations remain immutable; verify prior-data transitions.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Export through /api/archive; restore valid held records and reject malformed references/storage failures without losing prior data. Inspect event, incident, runbook, action, timeline and attribution identity and confirm authentication material is absent.

- **OC-008 — User-authored records kept.** No automatic deletion of user-authored incident records. Owner export/restore is supported. Ingress accepts bounded structured fields, no arbitrary raw body storage; native Events API request bytes max 64 KiB. Incumbent-compatible endpoints and provider adapters use the larger per-provider bounds of OC-059, and an authenticated over-limit request still raises a paging alert under OC-059 rather than only a refusal. No arbitrary remote fetching or runbook execution. Display user content as text, never HTML.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Submit over-limit and malformed intake, hostile structured text and runbook links; verify bounded refusal, literal display with no script execution/fetch, and preservation of authored history after restart/export.

- **OC-009 — Accessible interface.** Keyboard-complete responsive UI, labelled fields, focus-visible controls, status announcements, readable failure states and contrast. The conformance bar is WCAG 2.2 Level AA for every web surface and, for the OC-029 apps, the same success criteria as they apply to native iOS/Android plus Dynamic Type and Android font scaling to 200%. Named assistive-technology journeys are VoiceOver with Safari on macOS and NVDA with Chrome on Windows for web, and VoiceOver on iOS and TalkBack on Android for the apps; each completes sign-in, finding an incident in the filtered list (OC-060), acknowledge, snooze, reassign, schedule view and override. Each release publishes a conformance report (VPAT 2.5 Accessibility Conformance Report, WCAG 2.2 edition) stating the result per success criterion and per surface; an unsupported criterion is named, never omitted. A failed write is visible and never shown as saved. UI unavailable must not be described as pager coverage failure because stage 1 supplies no pager.

  **Acceptance evidence:** At 1440 and 390, use keyboard-only declaration, acknowledgement, notes, schedules, overrides and preview; inspect focus/labels, status announcements, literal hostile HTML and a real stale-write failure with no saved claim. Run axe-core with the WCAG 2.2 A and AA rule sets on every surface at 1440 and 390 in light and dark themes and record zero violations; complete the named VoiceOver/Safari, NVDA/Chrome, iOS VoiceOver and Android TalkBack journeys for acknowledge, snooze, reassign and schedule view with recorded outcomes; and publish the release's conformance report before any new target surface ships.

- **OC-010 — Health and honest commands.** Health checks storage and identifies the deployed version, operating commands describe actual local and hosted effects, and acceptance records distinguish local evidence from remote/provider/pilot outcomes. Capacity promises require measured evidence.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run check/describe and /health with storage available and unavailable; compare deployed identity to the actual build. Record local, hosted, provider and human-drill outcomes separately and test durable failure diagnostics for each new execution class.

## Admission and authority refinements after independent review

The public intake request keys, authentication modes, field limits, service-ID lookup and a curl example are documented in [Incidents and alerts](docs/incidents.md#send-an-alert-event).

Identity is source + eventId; conflicts compare the normalized allowlisted signal fields (canonical timestamps and trimmed strings), rather than irrelevant JSON whitespace/order or discarded fields. Receipts, admission order, decision snapshot and alert mutation commit atomically. Each signed/bearer source is explicitly scoped to service identities; the service binds its environment. On the native Events API, lifecycle generation is required: trigger and recovery must carry the same generation. Incumbent-compatible endpoints whose payloads carry no generation (OC-055) never require one: Oncall derives it from the source's deduplication key and receipt order, as OC-055 specifies, and the same tombstone and reopening rules then apply. Recovery before trigger creates a recovered tombstone; later trigger for that generation records evidence without reopening. Reopening needs a new generation. A delayed recovery can only close its own generation, never a new alert.

Stage 1 routes coordination ownership, with no timed escalation or paging. Replay returns historical decision/ownership/policy/evaluation snapshot beside the proposed current decision in submitted order; it does not claim schedule replay. All active members see all workspace incident data. Admin/responder/viewer permissions differ from incident commander attribution. Any active responder/admin can attest recovery and follow-up evidence; attestation is a human claim, not automatic measurement verification. Archive and restore require current admin authority; handoffs remain internal. Restore replaces application state while preserving the current administrator identity; credentials remain installation-local, other sessions are revoked, clients reload state. Historical and inactive identities are retained.

## Scheduling and held history

- **OC-011 — Calendar-day rotations.** Named-zone calendar-day rotations retain fixed primary/backup member slots. Hand-offs use the wall minute plus integer civil days, not fixed elapsed seconds. Repeated minutes explicitly choose earlier, later or reject; nonexistent/rejected boundaries produce visible gaps. Override intervals include their start and exclude their end; overlap in the same role is rejected. Inactive/viewer/missing members do not shift slots. A duplicated primary/backup assignment leaves backup unavailable.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Save primary/backup schedules and query HTTP preview across spring/fall DST, ambiguous/nonexistent minutes, override edges and overlap refusal; retain inactive/viewer/missing slots as gaps and detect duplicate primary/backup responders.

- **OC-012 — Paging off.** Production paging is OFF. Every normal-installation delivery attempt refuses with an explanation that an independent standby and real-phone drill are required. No production paging provider sends, synthetic delivery proof, cron delivery, SMS, voice or push are performed. Existing pager is authoritative. Escalation policies are inspectable configuration data, not active timers. A labelled demo simulation is not production delivery or acceptance.

  **Acceptance evidence:** On a normal installation POST each delivery/job/tick/callback/resume door and confirm paging-OFF refusal with no receipt. Treat demo simulations as simulations. Native cutover additionally requires OC-026–029 and OC-038 real-phone/provider-failure acceptance.

- **OC-013 — Held history stays historical.** Held synthetic job/receipt/callback identities remain historical data. Restore pauses pending jobs. Historical acknowledgement and explicit cancellation retain attribution; resume and new timer starts refuse while paging is OFF. An acknowledgement never proves recovery.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Restore held synthetic jobs/receipts/callbacks, observe pending work paused, acknowledge or cancel with attribution, reject resume, and verify incident recovery remains unchanged.

- **OC-014 — Side-effect-free previews.** Schedule preview, now/next and replay are side-effect-free; actual historical delivery claims remain labelled synthetic. Nothing in this release certifies production delivery.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Read now/next and schedule/routing previews, compare durable state and receipt counts, and confirm no timers or provider actions were created and historical synthetic data stays labelled.

- **OC-015 — Strict module archives.** Strict module archive validation and atomic replacement remain supported. Imported pending historical jobs pause. Scope and local capacity limits remain explicit.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Round-trip the strict module archive through protected HTTP; reject unknown fields, broken references and injected SQL failure atomically, retain callbacks and pause pending historical jobs.

## Consistent recovery checkpoint target

- **OC-016 — Recovery checkpoint.** A paired recovery checkpoint captures coordination and scheduling records with both revisions in one consistent storage snapshot, excludes authentication, and validates its envelope and intrinsic record integrity. Explicit current-admin restore replaces both datasets atomically, pauses imported pending jobs, preserves the current-password mapping rule and revokes prior authority exactly as coordination restore does. Missing references in retained paused/finished history remain evidence, never dispatch authority; missing references for pending jobs are invalid. Invalid input, capacity failures, storage failure or losing concurrent writes preserve the previous complete state and cannot revoke a winning restore's new session. Verify the actual emitted Worker/D1 and private local CLI workflow before claiming this target implemented.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use /api/recovery/checkpoint and the private CLI to capture and restore both revisions; exercise malformed/capacity/storage failures and competing writes, identity mapping, orphan history and session revocation without revoking the winning restore session.

## Retained reviewed handoffs

- **OC-017 — Reviewed handoffs.** Persist explicitly authored, unsent handoff copy with an explicit intended audience, immutable incident/service snapshots and canonical content hashes. Preparation and review actions have stable identities that conflict on changed content. Only current active responders/admins prepare, review or withdraw; viewers inspect permitted internal records and admins restore. A review binds exact copy and source versions, becomes ineligible on source drift or a recovery hold, and never grants sending, publication, recovery verification or independent-review authority. Withdrawal is terminal and preserves attributed append-only history. Keep OC-006 and historical unreviewed envelopes unchanged. Use separate additive handoff storage/archive bounded to 64 records, 128 history entries per record, 128 KiB per record and 1 MiB aggregate. Explicit checkpoint version 2 includes handoffs; version 1 remains supported. Restore retains historical identities and holds active approvals atomically alongside existing recovery and credential/session safeguards. Invalid input, capacity/storage failure or losing concurrent writes preserve the complete prior state. HANDOFF-STAGE.md defines retained snapshots, eligibility and permissions; transfer proof exercises the protected HTTP review and restore flow. No provider send, remote fetch or Lamp publication is authorized.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare, review, inspect and export exact handoff copy over protected HTTP; change source/revision, withdraw, restore v1/v2 and race writes. Verify eligibility/holds, stable action identities, append-only attribution and all storage bounds with no send authority.

## Release scope — owner direction, 16 October 2026

Release 1 is hosted incident coordination alongside the team's existing pager. Paging stays OFF, the existing pager stays authoritative, and coordination does not require an independent standby. Hosting does not synchronize the pager's configuration, deliver pages, announce role moves externally or prove delivery. Teams keep their existing pager routes and responder notification settings in place.

Release 2 paging and its independent standby are not built. Its requirements include dual alert intake, configuration pull, independent store/secrets/identity and external role-move announcements; durable absolute deadlines and term-scoped sent markers; one-use acknowledgement at both sites; two channels on two providers per responder; carrier registration; and real-phone and provider-failure drills. Measured takeover-to-page time belongs in this contract only after those drills. None is inferred from coordination deployment or local proof.

Business services built on these dependencies, with stakeholder subscriptions and an internal status view, are OC-061; each service's recent deploys and configuration changes are OC-065.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Create two teams and a dependent service through the UI/API, read the alert runbook, import a Backstage ownership change, deny a foreign-team user through direct/export/chat doors, and resolve an ownership conflict without losing incident history.

- **OC-024 — Follow-the-sun, PTO and holiday coverage.** Compose primary/secondary layers with daily, weekly, custom interval and business-hour rotations across named zones.   **Acceptance evidence:** Build New York/Los Angeles/London handoffs, add a US holiday and approved PTO in Google and Outlook, inspect a DST weekend and uncovered interval, then apply a reviewed layer change and compare actual paging targets to the preview.

- **OC-026 — Native US phone, SMS, push and email paging.** Every responder enrolls and verifies real destinations and keeps two separate ordered notification rule sets, one for high urgency and one for low urgency (OC-057), each by channel, on/off-call state and delay in minutes; a rule with zero delay notifies immediately. The two-channel/two-provider requirement below applies to the high-urgency set; the low-urgency set may be, for example, push and email only. Deliver SMS and voice using Twilio and an independently operated second provider such as Telnyx, plus mobile push through APNs/FCM and incident email through a configured US mail service (Resend, SendGrid or Amazon SES). Require at least two channels over two independent providers per responder, including a telephone route independent of the mobile push path. Complete US A2P 10DLC registration, sender/caller identity, consent/opt-out handling and carrier testing before activation. Opt-out, invalid number, unregistered sender or failed provider leaves coverage degraded and pages an approved alternate; it never silently counts as delivered. Show queued, attempted, provider-accepted, device/carrier-delivered where observable, human-confirmed and explicitly acknowledged separately, with provider costs/usage in USD. Intake-to-first-provider-attempt and per-channel provider-acceptance latency meet the OC-064 bars.

  **Acceptance evidence:** On registered US carrier phones, trigger a real alert and verify voice/DTMF, SMS replies/links, push and incident email, with intake-to-first-attempt measured against the OC-064 bar; send a low-urgency alert and verify only the responder's low-urgency rules fire. Disable Twilio then the second provider and the push path separately; prove an independent route reaches the responder. Reject forged/duplicate callbacks, expose uncertainty and opt-out, and record actual receipts and human acknowledgement rather than test-provider success.

- **OC-027 — Durable escalation until acknowledged.** Versioned service escalation policies use absolute persisted deadlines, scheduled target resolution at firing time, simultaneous/ordered levels, primary/secondary targets, repeats (0–9 passes through the policy), round-robin, explicit reassignment and manual escalation. Each level's escalation delay is a whole number of minutes from 1 to 1,440. The acknowledgement timeout is the per-service re-trigger of OC-058, and snooze is OC-058's durable snooze; neither exists only in a browser. Incoming eligible alerts start a durable obligation without a browser open. Each obligation records owner, due time, attempts, stop condition and terminal/failure outcome. The stop conditions are exactly: acknowledgement, source recovery of the paging generation (OC-056), resolution, explicit cancellation by an authorized human, and the urgency rule of OC-057 (low urgency does not escalate unless the service opts in); each stop records which condition fired. Acknowledgement atomically records responsibility and stops future escalation at both sites; resolution remains OC-004. Deadlines fire within the OC-064 lateness bar and never early. Reassignment visibly transfers responsibility and starts the configured receiving deadline. No eligible target, exhausted repeats, expired acknowledgement or failed transport escalates to the designated final responder and leaves an operator-visible failure; silence never means handled. Duplicate workers/callbacks cannot consume a round-robin turn twice or restart a stopped job.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Trigger through the Events API, close the browser, cross a handoff and observe primary, secondary, repeat and round-robin pages, each first attempt within the OC-064 deadline-lateness bar of its persisted due time (a 5-minute escalation fires between 5:00 and 5:30 at p99) and none early. Recover the source before the first escalation deadline and observe no further notification and a recorded stopped-by-recovery outcome. Restart workers at a deadline; race reassignment, acknowledgement, duplicate callbacks and acknowledgement expiry. Inspect one consistent obligation history, stopped jobs and the final failure path.

Announce role moves externally to operators, record configuration age/backlog, and expose which site owns each obligation. Provider timeouts remain uncertain; do not claim impossible exactly-once carrier delivery. Measure loss-to-takeover and takeover-to-first-provider-attempt times against the OC-064 bars (standby authority within 120 seconds of loss; first attempt for every due obligation within 60 seconds of takeover), verify fencing, role announcements and recovery/failback, and prove no acknowledged incident resumes. - **OC-059 — Large and partly readable payloads still page.** Request bounds follow each provider rather than one global cap. Incumbent-compatible endpoints (OC-055) accept at least 512 KiB, PagerDuty's documented maximum. The Prometheus Alertmanager, Datadog and Grafana adapters (OC-030) and SNS (OC-031) accept at least 2 MiB per request, and they split a grouped notification into one event per alert, subject to that adapter's field bounds. Over-long labels, annotations and details are cut with a visible truncation marker and the original byte count, and the alert itself is never dropped. The native Events API keeps its 64 KiB bound (OC-008). Suppose a request exceeds its endpoint's bound, or cannot be fully parsed, but its URL or key authenticates it to one service. Oncall then still does three things. It creates a high-urgency "incomplete intake" alert on that service from whatever fields it could read, with a count of alerts and bytes not read. It pages that service under its policy. It notifies the integration owner. It retains bounded structured evidence and no raw body: byte total, SHA-256, the readable alert identities and the dropped count. It returns 202 with a truncation flag, never a bare 413. A request that cannot be authenticated to a service is refused and shown to integration owners as a rejection diagnostic.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Send a 300 KiB Alertmanager group of 200 alerts, and observe 200 alert events, a page and no refusal. Send a 600 KiB PagerDuty v2 event and a 3 MiB Alertmanager group. Observe an incomplete-intake alert that pages the service, an integration-owner notice, bounded evidence of what was dropped, and a 202 response with the truncation flag. Send an oversize unauthenticated request, observe a refusal diagnostic and no alert, and confirm no raw body is stored.

- **OC-060 — Incident triage list: search, filters, "mine" and bulk actions.** The alert and incident list offers four kinds of filter. **Search:** full-text search over title, summary, service, deduplication key and notes. **Status and severity:** status (triggered, acknowledged, snoozed, resolved), urgency, priority and severity. **Ownership:** service, team, assignee, "assigned to me" and "my teams". **Time:** created and updated time range. The list sorts by time, urgency or priority. Filters are shareable by URL, users can save them, and the same queries are available on the mobile apps (OC-029) and the API (OC-053), with cursor pagination. Team privacy (OC-023) hides foreign items from every door. Bulk acknowledge, snooze, reassign, set urgency or priority, merge and resolve apply to up to 500 selected items, or to "all matching" up to 1,000 after a confirmed count. Bulk resolve closes alert-only items. For declared incidents it requires one OC-004 summary and evidence, recorded and attributed on each incident. Each item commits independently, with its own authority and revision check. The result reports success or the reason for failure per item, and every item's timeline carries a shared bulk operation ID, so a replay changes nothing twice.

  **Acceptance evidence:** During a 50-incident storm, filter by team, service, urgency and status, view "assigned to me" on web and mobile, and acknowledge all 50 in one action. Each incident's timeline shows the bulk operation ID. A viewer's bulk attempt is refused for every item. A stale revision on one incident fails visibly while the other 49 commit. Replay the operation and observe no second change. Bulk-resolve declared incidents without a summary and observe refusal. Confirm that a foreign team's incidents are neither listed nor selectable.

- **OC-061 — Business services, stakeholder subscriptions and internal status.** Model business services, the capabilities customers and the company recognize (for example "Checkout"), on top of the OC-023 technical-service dependencies. A business service is impacted when a supporting service has an open declared incident, or a high-urgency alert where the rule says so. Its internal status (operational, degraded or major outage) is derived from that impact, and the communications owner can set it by hand with a recorded reason. Internal users subscribe themselves to business services or teams, or admins and communications owners subscribe them. Subscribers can be users, teams, Slack/Teams channels and email lists, and each chooses email, push (OC-029) or chat delivery. Approved internal updates (OC-051) and optional templated "impact started/ended" notices reach subscribers of the impacted business services, and raw alert text is never sent. A **stakeholder** role, assignable to SSO/SCIM users (OC-039) and separate from viewer/responder/admin, sees only the internal status view and the approved updates for business services it may see. It is denied incident timelines, alerts, runbooks, responder contacts and chat channels. The internal status view is distinct from Lamp's public status pages and never publishes externally.

  **Acceptance evidence:** A stakeholder subscribes to "Checkout". An incident on a supporting service turns "Checkout" degraded on the internal status view. The stakeholder is notified only through the approved update, by their chosen channel, and is denied the incident timeline, alerts and runbook over web, mobile, API and export doors. An unsubscribed stakeholder receives nothing. A private team's business service is invisible to stakeholders outside its scope. A manual status override is attributed.

- **OC-062 — Configuration as code with Terraform and OpenTofu.** Oncall publishes and maintains a Terraform/OpenTofu provider on the Terraform and OpenTofu registries, built on the OC-053 API. It has resources and data sources for teams and memberships, schedules with layers and restrictions, escalation policies, services, urgency and support-hours rules, integrations (keys are write-only and sensitive in state), business services and dependencies, stakeholder subscriptions, maintenance windows, event orchestration rules, library actions (OC-063) and responder notification rules. Destinations still need the responder's own verification under OC-026. `plan` calls a dry-run preview endpoint that returns the same coverage preview as the UI (who gains or loses coverage, OC-024). `apply` refuses if the server's preview no longer matches the plan. Existing objects are adoptable through import blocks. Objects managed by the provider are labelled in the UI and API, and an administrator chooses per workspace whether UI edits on them are blocked or allowed and shown as drift. Either way, the next `plan` reports the drift. A converter takes a module using the official PagerDuty Terraform provider ([PagerDuty/terraform-provider-pagerduty](https://github.com/PagerDuty/terraform-provider-pagerduty): `pagerduty_team`, `pagerduty_team_membership`, `pagerduty_user`, `pagerduty_user_contact_method`, `pagerduty_user_notification_rule`, `pagerduty_schedule`, `pagerduty_escalation_policy`, `pagerduty_service`, `pagerduty_service_integration`, `pagerduty_business_service`, `pagerduty_service_dependency`, `pagerduty_maintenance_window` and `pagerduty_event_orchestration*`) or the Opsgenie provider, and emits Oncall resources and a mapping report. The report names each unconvertible attribute and does not approximate, matching OC-021.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Convert a PagerDuty Terraform module with schedules, layers, an escalation policy, services, integrations and a business service, and read its mapping report. `plan` shows the intended diff and the coverage preview, and `apply` produces the same configuration the UI preview showed. Edit a managed object out of band in the UI, and observe drift in the next `plan`, or a refusal if edits are locked. Apply a stale plan and observe refusal. Confirm integration keys never appear in plan output.

- **OC-063 — Curated automation actions and named executors.** Admins and team managers curate a library of diagnostic and remediation actions. Each action has a name, description, kind, executor, typed and bounded parameters, target services, allowed teams and roles, an optional second-person approval, a timeout, an output limit and output redaction rules. **Named executors:** (1) a self-hosted runner, an open-source agent on the customer's infrastructure that polls over outbound HTTPS, runs pinned scripts and needs no inbound port; (2) AWS Systems Manager Run Command and Automation documents, through a cross-account role with an external ID; (3) GitHub Actions `workflow_dispatch`, through a GitHub App scoped to named repositories; (4) Kubernetes `rollout restart` and `rollout undo` of named deployments, through the runner with namespace-scoped RBAC; and (5) Rundeck / PagerDuty Runbook Automation job triggers, so existing jobs keep working. Responders run allowed actions from the incident on web, mobile and Slack/Teams. Each run records actor, parameters, executor, start, finish and exit status, and streams bounded, redacted output to the incident timeline. A timeout or a lost runner is recorded as an uncertain outcome and is never retried blindly. Credentials are scoped to each run and never enter archives, logs or AI context (OC-042). Viewers, users outside the allowed teams and items outside the target services are refused. PagerDuty Automation Actions definitions import as inactive library entries with a mapping report (OC-038). Agent plans (OC-048) can use only library actions within their bounds.

  **Acceptance evidence:** A responder runs a library diagnostic from Slack, and its bounded output appears in the incident timeline with attribution. A viewer is refused, and so is a responder outside the allowed team. Run a Kubernetes rollout restart through the runner and a GitHub Actions dispatch, then kill the runner mid-run and observe an uncertain outcome with no automatic rerun. An agent plan restricted to one named action cannot run another. Import a PagerDuty Automation Actions export and observe inactive entries with a mapping report.

- **OC-064 — Stated paging timing bars and reference load.** Paging is measured against fixed bars at a named US reference load. **Reference load:** 500 responders, 100 teams, 1,500 services and 3,000 integration keys; a sustained 600 accepted events per minute for one hour; a burst of 12,000 events within 60 seconds, carrying 1,000 distinct alert generations; and 1,000 open paging obligations, with 200 escalation deadlines due in the same minute. **Pass bars at the burst:** (1) intake receipt returned at p99 ≤ 2 seconds, with no accepted event lost; (2) intake to first provider attempt for a high-urgency, zero-delay rule at p95 ≤ 10 seconds and p99 ≤ 30 seconds; (3) deadline lateness (actual first attempt minus persisted due time, for escalation, repeat, snooze and timeout deadlines) at p95 ≤ 15 seconds, p99 ≤ 30 seconds and maximum ≤ 60 seconds, never early; (4) no notification attempt started more than 5 seconds after an acknowledgement or recovery commits; (5) provider acceptance at p95 ≤ 5 seconds after each attempt for every channel; and (6) standby authority within 120 seconds of main-site loss, and a first attempt for every due obligation within 60 seconds of takeover (OC-028). Escalation delays run from 1 to 1,440 minutes (OC-027). Carrier and device delivery after provider acceptance is measured and published, never promised. Measured takeover values come only from the OC-028 drills. A release that misses a bar publishes the miss in the OC-054 matrix and cannot pass the cutover checklist.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Drive the reference load through the public intake doors, with provider fakes only at the paid network edge for timing and a real-phone sample for end-to-end receipt. Measure p50, p95, p99 and maximum for intake receipt, intake to first attempt, deadline lateness, stop-after-acknowledgement and provider acceptance, and compare each with its bar. Confirm that no deadline fires early and that a 5-minute escalation fires between 5:00 and 5:30 at p99. Publish the measured values in the OC-054 readiness matrix.

- **OC-065 — Change events and recent changes for responders.** Oncall accepts change events that never page, through the PagerDuty Change Events API (`POST /v2/change/enqueue` with `routing_key`, `summary`, `timestamp`, `source`, `custom_details` and `links`), a native change API, GitHub deployment, release and merged-pull-request webhooks, GitLab deployment webhooks, and generic signed webhooks from Argo CD, Terraform Cloud and CI jobs. Each change attaches to its service. A **recent changes** panel on every service, alert and incident, on web and mobile and in the Slack/Teams incident summary, lists the changes for that service and its direct dependencies within a lookback window. The window defaults to 24 hours before the trigger and can be set from 1 to 168 hours. Each entry shows time, author, source link and whether the change belongs to a dependency. Humans see it without any agent. OC-045 reads the same records. Change records follow team privacy (OC-023), retention and erasure (OC-040) and archives (OC-052). Their links grant no access.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Send a change through the PagerDuty-compatible change endpoint and a GitHub deployment webhook, one for the alerting service and one decoy for an unrelated service, plus one for a dependency. Trigger the alert and observe that no page is caused by any change event. The recent-changes panel lists the service's and the dependency's changes, labelled, and omits the unrelated decoy. A user without access to the service sees none of them. Replay a change event and observe one record.
